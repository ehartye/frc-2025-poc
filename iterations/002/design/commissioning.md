## Match and battery decision

The 20 ms trace replays the signed pan torque and speed from four previously measured source runs. It lays the newly scheduled wheel motion and six finite azimuth changes on the same clock. At the two nominal docks there is 1.558 and 1.443 seconds left after the 250 ms settle and before tip starts. The first adverse schedule arrives 31 and 114 ms late. Raising only straight-line cruise from 1.55 to 1.70 m/s gives 179 and 76 ms of margin, with a 10.04 V minimum bus on the 11.8 V, 32 mΩ source. Drive demand stays below 16.1 A per motor in those modeled runs. A 20 ms actuation delay and 1.50 m/s² braking at 1.70 m/s require approximately 0.997 m to stop.

The remaining 76 ms is time available for unmodeled disturbances. Actual four-corner tracking, steering friction, mass, battery, wires, new acceleration effects on the pipe, field occupancy, sensors and reset can consume it. A slower historical physical leg took 25.195–25.205 s. The new schedule still requires an integrated loaded run in both sectors. The drive’s modeled voltage margin does not cover a 43.9 A nominal pan demand at a 30 A limit.

Use the original start, supply, timestamps and five peer footprints for the integrated test. Credit each identified pipe only after release and sustained field support. If stopping, carrying and sensing fail on either trip, compare the simple fixed-path architecture before detailing another upper. The new chassis is independently reusable while those results remain open.
