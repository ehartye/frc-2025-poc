# Receiver delivery load and secondary suspension

The registered bumper robot carries an estimated **8.422–11.609 kg including one 0.816 kg pipe**. The estimate is a bill-of-materials and routing bound. The original lift study used 12.585 kg on a superseded 96-body design. The 401-body audit visits all 174 lift, middle, slide, variable-rope and draw-wire bodies. Surviving structural BOM entries contribute 5.608 kg. The actuator BOM contributes 1.959–2.969 kg. Eleven previously unmapped tabs, lines, pads, sensor boards and harness anchors contribute 0.039–0.215 kg by allowance. The upper estimate also assigns the full 1.65 kg project wiring allowance and 0.35 kg of incidental moving hardware to the carriage. Count those allocations once in a whole-robot budget. An instrumented weight remains outstanding. [Load ledger by part](delivery-retention.json)

## What carries a failed line

A separate candidate adds one backup haul line per side at |Y| = 292 mm around the existing pair at 252 mm. Each has a dedicated drum, top sheave and bracket, eye splice, clevis, pin and carriage attachment. The four drums pay out parallel single layers on a common 1/2-inch hex shaft. The shaft extends to 640 mm and gains two outboard supports. The 380 mm stroke fits 26.924 mm working width with two retained dead wraps on the matched 19.05 mm cores. Keep the primary/backup lengths and pitch radii paired when setting the stop positions.

The backup clevis mounts below the crossbar on its own 12 mm plate and two M6 through-bolts at |Y| = 214 and 232 mm. The top hanger attaches to the forward mast face on separate fasteners. Sleeved M5 through holes transmit load through both mast walls. The outboard bearing is 22 mm past its drum. The bore/pin, eye and cylindrical line solids reserve termination space. Source a finished splice, grooved sheave, hex bearing, axial shaft retention, guarded drums and sheave keepers matched to the actual rope and winding direction. A supplier catalog core dimension alone cannot establish rope seating, eye strength or effective winding radius. [Original editable layer](duplex.py) and [reopened placement STEP](duplex-place.step)

During a single primary line break, clevis loss or independent drum-anchor failure, the corresponding backup takes up the bounded slack and holds through its own anchors; the intact side remains supported. Loss of one backup leaves its primary intact. The model assigns the whole moving mass to one loaded backup. A parked, supported robot can also transfer its load to two removable access-controlled 8 mm service pins. The pins pass through independent fixed rails at the three operating heights. Their upper surface is 1 mm below each carriage plate before settling. Install and retain both pins before removing line tension; a storage/withdrawn indication and positive lock against unintended insertion are needed. Retain the removable pins with a positive clip. Join each 6061 service rail to its two separate arms with inspected welds or an engineered bolted joint. The butt joints in the fit model expose the joint locations. Weld throat, heat-affected strength, local pin contact, keeper access and actual deformation require proof. Common shaft, gearbox, ratchet, crossbar or mast failure during travel has no independent arrest proof. The service pins require external insertion and cannot authorize powered travel or automatic recovery.

## Conditional sizing and rejected alternatives

The added moving parts and 0.25 kg allowance give a **12.173 kg loaded upper estimate**. Fixed components add an estimated 2.182 kg including 0.35 kg for guards, retainers and hardware. Machining the stationary service rails from aluminum and using six steel pin bushings saves 1.193 kg against solid steel rails. The 12 mm rail is 18 mm wide, with an 8.2 mm pin bore in a 12 mm outside-diameter sleeve. Qualify sleeve fit, axial retention and bearing stress before service use. Include both fixed and moving additions before comparing overall mass, center of gravity, traction, field clearance or inspection margin. This prototype changes the input assembly for every registered mission qualification.

With the shaft holding position, a starting descent of 0.253 m/s and 1.460 mm total differential slack, an effective line-plus-termination stiffness of 90–120 N/mm yields a 5.68–6.57 mm maximum one-side drop and a 460–506 N peak line reaction. Shoe lateral demand is 0.65–0.76 mm across the 58 mm shoe length, leaving the design's 0.2 mm tolerance reserve in the 1 mm guide clearance. The simple spring-energy bound assumes no extra winch payout and a bounded force/displacement curve. Measure the complete rope circuit, gearbox lash, guide clearance, loaded twist, release response, load centroid, rebound and mount compliance. A softer 40 N/mm circuit exceeds the available shoe clearance. Mixed 0.750/0.787-inch cores diverge by another 16.07 mm over the stroke. Neither control passes.

Allocate 0.30 mm initial length-setting error, no more than 0.012 mm paired effective radius error, 0.45 mm differential bedding/stretch and 0.30 mm fleet/wear variation. Match **loaded payout at both ends and along the stroke**. Reject a pair that exceeds 1.460 mm at any sample, including backlash reversal, creep and load cycling. A 90–120 N/mm measured effective stiffness bracket is mandatory for the numeric catch claim; a stiffer circuit needs a higher peak force review.

The 506 N peak implies 1.013 kN return-sheave reaction and 5.86 Nm at the larger drum radius for the one-line case. The conservative circular shaft bending screen is 42.4 MPa, the 23 × 12 mm aluminum mounting neck is 71.5 MPa, and the carriage bolt couple adds 1.69 kN. A single 8 mm service pin supporting twice the whole static load at 40 mm cantilever sees approximately 190 MPa. Verify fastener preload, thread engagement, slip, combined shear/tension, net section, local bearing, impact, fatigue, shaft torque plus bending, bracket welds and actual alloy. The full drive train needs rated hold through the larger of normal locked gravity and emergency loading. The old 2.86 Nm static input does not cover this single-line peak. The independently braked rod catcher alternative requires an exact maker-rated head, minimum release tension, stroke, dedicated mounting and rod attachment. The available KRM family information does not settle those selection questions. [Mechanical rupture-triggered catcher](https://www.sitema.de/wp-content/uploads/datenblaetter/en/sonder/SITEMA-TI-KRM10-EN.pdf)

The modeled scheduled two-second return and loaded receive-to-carry moves still fit the 9.5 V, 35 A motor-side envelope. The rejected one-second return exceeds the voltage budget. The revised screen adds the extra shaft length, drums, sheaves and 100 g rotating-hardware allowance at bounding radii to the original motor-side inertia. Confirm actual inertia, loaded bus voltage and brake thermal performance.

## Shared shaft failure

A failed shaft or pawl can unwind all four lines while they stay taut. Neither a rope-slack switch nor a tension-triggered rod clamp can reliably detect that event. At maximum loaded mass and initial descent speed, a shaft free to unwind through 380 mm admits a 2.74 m/s bottom arrival and 45.75 J. A hypothetical linear buffer with 20 mm stroke would see 4.81 kN peak. No such qualified bumper or arrest mechanism is present.

An independent speed follower attached between the actual carriage and stationary mast could trip a spring-applied rail brake with the motor, shaft and control power absent. A proposed 0.35 m/s threshold is above the planned 0.253 m/s descent. At 10 ms delay and 2 g net deceleration the minimum modeled stopping distance is 12.09 mm. At 50 ms it grows to 50.74 mm. These numbers exclude installation play, notch indexing, rod deflection and brake engagement variation. The bottom of stroke needs a mechanically separate stop or a proven reduced-speed approach. The PFB LK200 architecture demonstrates an independent governor family, but the governor alone supplies no clamp, admissible robot mass or integration rating. Record the actual independent trip speed, latency, arrest force, clearance, supplier drawing, environmental limits and reset behavior before claiming in-motion common-shaft protection. [PFB product information](https://www.pfb.it/en/product/3264/overspeed-governors/lk-200-bidirectional)

The four-pose integration inventory identifies each body, kinematic frame, uniform-density mass and centroid. Collect the fixed and moving supplementary allowances separately. Apply `machine_existing(name, shape, height)` to the shop-machined crossbar and masts to retain both sets of holes when building a successor. [Layer mass and pose inventory](duplex-inventory.json)

## Evidence required before operation

| Check | Pass observation |
| --- | --- |
| Build identity | Weigh stripped and populated carriage at all moving poses; record installed wire and hose routing, fasteners and calibrated payload. Every moving article maps to a ledger entry, and measured mass with uncertainty is inside its release budget. |
| Secondary load path | Verify separate anchors and keyed drums on both sides, retaining pin security, measured loaded effective payout, correct winding, positive shaft retention, clear line guards and no shared fastener between the primary and secondary anchor. Inspect single-failure route to the anchored frame. |
| Static fixture | On a guarded, independently supported bench fixture, measure stiffness, 1.46 mm worst line-length tracking and full-load one-sided holding. Inspect the actual line, splices, pulleys, anchors, shaft, ratchet and beam against the above force plus design margin. Require independent engineering acceptance of actual proof load and fixture containment. |
| Failure and settling | With a captive test mass and secondary support, qualify both sides, minimum/maximum height, largest extension and worst loaded descent. Record actual shaft payout, time, acceleration, both shoe displacements, peak forces and remaining clearance. Reject twist, catch slip, renewed motion, loose anchors, excessive guide loading, or slide travel into an obstruction. |
