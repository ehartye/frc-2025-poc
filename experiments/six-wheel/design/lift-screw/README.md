**Keep the screw replacement at fixture-study status.** Of three single-start screw sizes, one covers the four scheduled lift moves and fits the registered robot. A screw that resists backdriving at rest can still coast past the low placement structure. The commercial drag unit examined also exceeds its speed limit on direct drive. Conditional reduced-speed drag settings exist. No tested friction range, supported bearing layout or coupled-failure arrest establishes a buildable qualified replacement.

## Coasting with both haul lines removed

The revised lower bearing/support reserve introduces the first measured contact for this proposal. The working sleeve has 5.95 mm axial clearance at placement. Independent solid intersection is zero through that gap, then reaches 25.03 mm³ per side at 6 mm of downward travel and 1,026.42 mm³ at 8 mm. The drag calculation therefore targets a 2.5 mm overrun, leaving 3.45 mm for this interface before mounting tolerance or elastic deflection. The separately relieved plate alone has more travel than the complete proposed stack.

The older winch permits the complete load to fall if a common drum shaft runs free. Twin screws convert this into a different fault. The local threads can support the load when the input or power is removed; two shafts, a motor and a translating carriage can already have kinetic energy when that happens. The model uses a 29° included Acme thread angle and an approximate pitch diameter, accounts for gravity in the lowering torque, and includes the bounded shaft inertias and reflected carriage mass. The coupled case retains an assumed 0.000075 kg·m² motor-side stack. The detached case removes that inertia. Brake-neutral electronics, guide friction and gearbox drag get no stopping credit.

The assumed static threshold is a coefficient of friction of 0.0770. At 0.05 the load can backdrive from rest. At 0.10 the unloaded 7.951 kg operating bound has positive holding torque but little net slowing torque. At 785.34 mm on the 940-to-670 mm descent, coupled coast is 396.26 mm under the inertia screen, **165.93 mm beyond the original fixed-arm boundary**. At 0.15 the same sweep has 4.09 mm minimum modeled surplus to that boundary. For the separate revised plate, both sides clear a simultaneous 9 mm fall at placement and overlap their lower fixed arms by 48 mm³ each at 10 mm. Moving the arm contact by a few millimeters cannot absorb the large adverse-friction case. Reported travel beyond impact is the unconstrained stopping demand. Actual earlier impact, payload/field clearance, ball-screw behavior and vibration-induced creep require separate evidence. [Numerical fault cases](screen.json) · [Supplier backdrive discussion](https://www.roton.com/screw-university/screw-actions/screw-backdriving-efficiency/)

A break in the actuator cross-drive can leave one screw supported and the other powered. With 510 mm screw separation, a 58 mm guide and 0.8 mm allowable shoe differential at its ends, only 7.03 mm left-right elevation difference is available. A scheduled 270 mm descent starting from rest commands more than this after **298 ms** if one nut stays locked. This describes kinematic incompatibility; a rigid carriage may bind or overload the bracket earlier. Use physically independent support on both sides plus a positive cross-coupling, jam response and a demonstrated reaction path. A second nut alone protects only a particular primary-nut/seat failure and shares the same screw, thrust bearing and mast.

## Always-engaged drag on each screw

Two passive rotary friction units, one per screw and reacted directly to the mast, could continue slowing the load with power and the upstream shaft absent. The model solves for the minimum pair drag needed to stop before H557.5 and the maximum pair drag the motor can overcome during the complete mission schedule. Both masses, 1,001 phases per move, positive/descent guide load, 95% transmission efficiency, a 0.000020 kg·m² additional screw-side inertia, 9.5 V and 35 A are included. The setting allowance includes ±20% installation/operating variation. Ranges describe conjectured effective thread friction; they require measurement.

The sweep covers 0.60–1.05 motor turns/screw turn at 0.01 increments and refines each coarse optimum at 0.0002 increments. Its torque upper limit conservatively combines unsigned acceleration and axle forcing; a failed upper-envelope screen rules out qualification by that envelope, rather than proving that every possible controller saturates. A qualified one-side load case, missing friction unit, or a slipping cross-drive remains outside the symmetric double-drag envelope. Public numerical output retains both negative and conditional positive controls.

Huco 401.25 is a 47 g, 25.8 × 34.4 mm, adjustable friction device with 6–8 mm clamp bore, 0.53 Nm maximum drag, 1,000 rpm limit and 80 °C maximum surface temperature. Direct drive would exceed that speed 3.59 times. With a separate nominal 4:1 speed reduction per spindle, each unit would turn at 897 rpm and need about 0.22 Nm ideal clutch drag for 0.11 Nm total at the screw pair. At maximum speed the pair must dissipate roughly 41 W; one full 760 mm mission motion cycle dissipates about 124 J across both. The reduction must remain connected downstream of the powered shaft failure and retain its gear, journal, reaction bracket and shaft connection. Verify real torque vs temperature, speed, breakaway, repeated cycling, bearing loads, reduction losses both directions and reflected inertia. Family catalog drag and temperature data do not establish continuous slip duty or gravitational support. Availability on the historical kickoff date is unverified. ## Inspection budget and decision

The 545 high R103 mass estimate leaves 0.89216 kg. A heavy 0.673205 kg wheel-carrier proposal leaves 0.21896 kg; the lighter 0.377621 kg proposal leaves 0.51454 kg. The prior terminal damper has a 0.50805 kg budget before attachment bolts. It leaves a 0.28909 kg deficit with the heavy carrier or only 0.00649 kg with the lighter carrier. These are alternate mechanical packages. The screw study gives no mass credit for deleting those uninstalled dampers.

Two 520 mm screws alone add 1.780 kg. Four bronze sleeve envelopes contribute an estimated 0.846 kg. Fitting this as an overlay fails both budgets. For a full replacement, retire the original 1.8–3.9 kg drive/ratchet/line allowance and 0.746 kg of specifically enumerated duplex parts, while retaining the relieved plates, service rails, pins, mast hardware and machining. With the heavy carrier, the prior high drive allowance would leave **2.239 kg** for the motor, transmission, thrust/radial bearings, brackets, fasteners, synchronization, independent drag hardware/guards and stronger accessories. If only 1.8 kg from the old drive is available, this shrinks to **0.139 kg**. The light carrier adds 0.296 kg to both figures. No supplier-ready complete BOM establishes the net mass. Separate surrendered and reused hardware on a scale before relying on any of the removed allowance. [545 mass record](../competition-package/shop-duplex/mass.json) · [Screw replacement ledger](screen.json)

**The next decision belongs to supplier data and a contained fixture.** Confirm speed, PV, both friction bounds, temperature and lubricant, journal machining, alignment and full-stroke duty of the selected bronze/screw pair. Obtain the continuous slip torque and allowable heat of the independent drag units. Record the as-built weighed bill and verify actual retained service-pin access. On an independently supported fixture, measure controlled stopping after loss of motor power, break in the cross-drive, one-side jam, broken primary nut and one missing brake at both mass extremes and adverse travel phases. Measure both nut positions, torque/force, local mast strain, speed, temperature, settled position and retained load. Reject a lost clearance or a recovered state that automatically resumes motion. The 545 field receipts and the historical 401 controller remain scoped to the original assemblies.
