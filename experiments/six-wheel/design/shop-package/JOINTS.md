## Outer bearing-carrier clamp · six identical assemblies

Cut one threaded saddle per outer carrier from 8 mm mild steel plate. The finished front elevation is 50.00 wide × 48.40 high, with a centered 26.00 wide opening from the top to 3.00 above the bottom. This leaves two 12.00 wide uprights and a connecting web. Use bottom and left edges as datums. Four tapped through M5×0.8 holes lie at (6.00, 7.20), (44.00, 7.20), (6.00, 41.20), (44.00, 41.20) mm. Drill Ø4.2 as the proposed cutting-tap pilot, chamfer starts, check with a thread gauge and verify the chosen tap's chart against the actual steel. Local upright edge distance is 6 mm center to edge. Break sharp non-bearing edges and exclude coatings or raised burrs from the clamp faces. In the assembly its bottom sits at Z52, its inside face at |Y|327. The exported threaded interface uses a suppressed Ø5 mm thread envelope.

From the wheel bay outward the clamping stack is an M5 × 16 socket head, 10 OD × 5.3 ID × 1 washer, 6 mm carrier, 2 mm inner tube wall, then 8 mm saddle. The screw engages 7 mm nominally and finishes 1 mm below the far face. Keeping all clamping within this stack avoids collapsing the full 25 mm tube. Per robot this detail adds six saddles, 24 screws and 24 washers. Proposed property class 8.8 screws and steel saddle stock are subject to actual certification, surface condition and torque/preload trials. The outer tube's far wall and original bumper exterior remain flush.

1. Slide saddles through the open outer tube ends with bumpers off and axles out. Confirm end access, weld penetration and actual internal corner radii with the production extrusion. A web with its bottom at the floor datum requires adequate edge relief on the two inner tube corners. Spot the ID fit on a tube coupon; preserve a flat load land behind every clamp.
2. Use a temporary M5 guide stud in the first slot to align each saddle; start the other screws by hand. Avoid letting the saddle fall loose in a closed/mobile chassis. Verify that the web clears the 17 mm shaft-retention tool corridor. Install the matching inner carriers, bearings, shafts, spacers and chain using the drivetrain sequence.
3. Back off the outer clamps just enough to adjust both bearing faces equally at the chain's tightest position. Apply a short 4 mm AF hex key between wheel/hub and screw heads. The native jig uses a 4.62 mm bounding diameter, handle plane |Y|310.60, 5.3 mm nib and 60 mm handle swinging 60° up and away from the axle. Compare the actual key and loosened head travel with the model before setting service torque. All 56 signed stroke/fastener checks cleared the 60 mm key model. The rejected 29 mm straight tool intersected wheel material by 1,468.69 mm³ at each location, and a 100 mm handle hit the end tube on a rear-outboard station. A full bolt withdrawal requires wheel/hub removal or equivalent opened service access.
4. Under the drive study's 616.11 N high-strand load with a 25 N slack-side allowance, an illustrative one-carrier longitudinal demand is 641.11 N if that single interface takes the entire sum. At assumed friction coefficients 0.15 / 0.10, four screws would need 1.07 / 1.60 kN clamp force each before impact or safety factor. The original 520 N outer bearing radial budget is a separate load component. Measure clamp slip/load, tube distortion, torque scatter, screw locking and repeatability. A rigid joint or confirmed working torque cannot be inferred from the drawing.

The four inner-center clamp screws on each gearbox side have a different closure: across the 6 mm center rail the existing gearbox faceplate is solid at X±19 and Z76.2±17. Ordinary backside nuts cannot fit between those faces. The four adjustable inner end carriers also require their own retained nuts or backed slots. Release those 24 inner positions after selecting a fastening detail and checking gearbox, bearing and chain tools.

## Receiver long-screw anchor · four assemblies

The existing shelf screw goes through the foot, riser, shelf and spacer into a 16 × 14 × 7 mm block resting in the open top of the 20 mm inner channel. The existing modeled Ø5.3 hole passes completely through that block and cannot retain an M5 thread. A proposed steel replacement uses the same finished bounding dimensions.

| Feature | Machining and check |
|---|---|
| Vertical thread | Center (8.00, 7.00) from rear and right stock faces. Drill Ø4.2 and tap M5×0.8 through the 7.00 height. Gauge after cross-drilling. Nominal screw grip from the head seat to thread entry is 40 mm; 46 mm underhead enters 6 mm, with 1 mm bottom clearance. The original Ø8.5 × 3 mm low head is a geometry allowance; select and verify the actual fastener and recess. |
| Two transverse dowels | Pin axes run along Y, at U=3.00 and 13.00 from the block's rear face, both 3.50 from its bottom. Use two Ø3 × 20 solid steel dowels per block. Hold by controlled interference in the **block only**. Ream to measured pins/steel coupon, with a provisional 0.004–0.014 mm diametral press target. Clearance holes through both 3 mm aluminum channel walls are Ø3.10 nominal. |
| Position in each 320 mm inner channel | Its rear end is X−160. Forward distances to pin axes are 75/85 mm for the rear block and 295/305 mm for the front block. Hole centers sit 3.50 below the upper sidewall edges. Fixture inside width 14 mm and keep both Ø3.10 wall bores coaxial within Ø0.08 at the mating axes; validate actual channel radii and wall thickness before drilling. Both blocks and both sides share the pattern. |
| Install and service | Fit and gauge the free inner stage before nesting. Use a backing fixture to keep the opposite wall from splaying. Ends must finish flush to nominal 20 mm outside width; use a straightedge and measure both sides before full-stroke travel. Remove the inner stage for future transverse-pin replacement. Use a positive bench restraint before separating any carriage. |
| Retention test | Exercise actual screw tightening, pullout, peel and repeated changes. With 1 kN assumed uplift on one block and two equally loaded pins in double shear, nominal steel shear is 35.4 MPa and 3 mm wall bearing is 27.8 MPa; one pin taking the load raises these to 70.7 and 55.6 MPa. The top wall ligament is only 1.95 mm at the Ø3.10 bore and the 3 mm pin lies 1.0 mm clear of the nominal M5 thread envelope. Verify tear-out, wall bending, steel net section and bore fatigue. |

The 1 kN uplift above is a design-screen input. The earlier receiver impact study tracks an **in-plane** force and torsional group load. Compare both sets of directions when the team defines a physical proof load. An accepted shop process, material certification, thread test, load test and on-robot alignment remain open.
