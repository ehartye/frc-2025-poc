Where does the robot’s weight land?

Three axles share the weight on each side. Move the center wheels up or change their stiffness to see whether the drive still meets its 75% end-axle design target.

Positive gap means the center wheel sits higher. Reference compression is how far six equal wheels would settle at this load. The calculation uses level ground, equal end-wheel stiffness, a centered load and linear springs that can push against the carpet. A real robot in a turn can also transfer load from side to side.

With 80% center stiffness, a 0.30 mm center gap and 1.00 mm reference compression, the end axles carry about 77%. The corrected station approach still passed at 77.5% in both tested battery cases. At 80% the 32 mΩ case reached its low-voltage inhibit. Read the signed drive results and assumptions.
