# Red 2 whole-robot decision

Advance the **sliding split-drop cradle** to a private native fixture. Both matched acquisition trials retained their pipe through the acceleration pulse. Its conditional drops reached the field support criterion in all 24 trials. The revised delivery shelf can remain above the trough while two supports move outward. That last operation is the most important unverified part of the design.

The task is two independently scored station-issued pipes during wall-clock seconds 18–153, followed by parking. All layouts use the central Red 2 start, the same source stock, the same five-peer traffic reference and fixed station/reef cameras. Coral A and B enter separate finite stock openings. The nominal feed times are 21.100 and 71.501 s, with release windows at 48.010 and 98.100 s. Scoring targets are the two L1 sectors at −180 and +180 mm. The adverse case uses the opposite wheel calibration, reduced entry friction and the recorded late-B reset sequence. The second adverse release window has no confirmed stow. The receiver must inhibit another feed until the loaded state or a clear reset has been observed.

The field support control intentionally omits projecting hardware and starts with a release state. The tipping-tray state comes from a finite rail/gate simulation. The roller and split-drop exits are bounded assumptions. The complete scan with the 370 mm tray held forward could not prove separation in any of 768 challenges. Consequently the comparison leaves the two recognized scores open for all three designs. No candidate has demonstrated a complete pickup, transit, release, separated assessment, reset and park with the other five robots.

The split-drop has a 3.4–8.2 kg class of candidate upper structures across the powered and sliding alternatives. Its own upper allocation is 4.1–8.2 kg. The modeled loaded split-drop CG ranges from −48.3 to −26.5 mm fore-aft and 232.5 to 265.8 mm above the carpet. These are design allowances. The loaded intake pulse reached 618 N nominal aggregate contact force in the receiver calculation. It motivates a two-sided load and shock case for the bearings, guide span, stop and attachments. Camera ray paths, battery lift, wiring and side-tool access appear on the editable blockouts.

The split-drop needs a longer custom carriage and more moving connections than the tipping tray. Its individual gates offer direct access to the two ends, but both slides and their home witnesses need maintenance. It ranks between the simpler fixed roller channel and the obstructed straight gate on residual design risk. Recover a known home state by inhibiting longitudinal travel while either support reports a disagreement. On tag loss retain the pipe, stop, reacquire a consistent reef pose, and use the reserved return path only after a clearance check. Give the operator time to park when a second completion cannot be established.

Reverse this selection if native sweep or dynamics show that the side-opening supports catch the pipe, threaten the field or cannot stow within 1 mm of the allocated forward envelope under tolerance. A short folding gate that passes the same trough intersection, integrated transfer and tool-access tests would make the faster-seating tipping tray competitive. The next fixture will test 404 mm fore-aft travel and a 90 mm outward stroke per side from the original 301.625 mm pipe length. The foundation must separately resolve its purchased module revision, motor/wheel sweeps and chassis attachments before any complete-robot review.
