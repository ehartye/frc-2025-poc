The two independently moving R2 supports and straight flat crossbars remain the comparison. The R3 model has unfinished carrier attachments, powered gearbox support, axial retention, end caps, lubrication, bilateral pipe centering and service access. The native shop timeline cuts both ends from the original supplier stock, leaves its real teeth and through holes, drills two each in the pull-bracket foot and web, and forms the guide/shoe profiles. Native kinematics drove both rails from one input through ±90 mm and rotated the pinion 8.85827 radians. The official rack and pinion were independently advanced through one full tooth pitch in 17 samples. At an extra 0.10 mm center spacing, all samples cleared and the smallest measured tooth gap was 0.05695 mm. The single-rack hold challenged 0.49873 mm of unlawful unilateral travel with the opposite rack held. Fusion measured 4.8347 mm³ of tooth interference on the left with the pinion locked. Sharing the correction equally between meshes still left 1.3041 and 1.3257 mm³ of interference. Material deflection, tooth failure and the actual drive limit remain separate physical requirements.

The worst one-jaw front load from the earlier signed shock screen is 439.96 N. Over a simple 480 mm transverse span with the jaw at Y116, the R2 flat section deflects 7.22 mm. R3's measured design section has a crosswise second moment of 36,466 mm⁴, giving 0.237 mm and 18.37 MPa under the same applied load. Centering all the load increases that deflection to 0.403 mm. The predicted running gap between shoe and lip is 0.6 mm before tolerance, misalignment or unsupported rail twist. Effective guide stiffness, actual wear and full-carriage load distribution remain open.

The matched centered pipe at z570 cleared all 72 native solids at the 89.09 ms witness. A second native trial placed the rigid pipe tangent to the two section slopes at X598.322877, Z609.012241 after presentation. Both native methods measured zero positive overlap. Actual station-to-seat dynamics and loaded recovery have to reach that state independently. An off-center pipe can lose one end before its partner even when the jaws move equally.

The full native R3 parked census found four 37.644 mm³ rack/screw clashes and two nut/bracket contacts. In the presented native state the nested jaw bolts remained at home while both racks and pull brackets moved 404 mm. The output adapter also stayed behind its pinion. These findings disqualify R3 for mechanical acceptance. Future checkpoints must measure the relative pose of every fixed component under motion, as well as world-vertex consistency and complete part-pair intersections.
